Disclaimer – I’ve never used Ozone 11 but I have listened on the web to examples of the assistant being used as was not impressed. I reckon if there comes a time when you can press a magic button and brilliant master is spat out the other end - you'll soon hear about it. Until then I’d ignore the hype. Okay an absolute newbie might glean some insight into what some of these modules do by dissecting what the assistant has spewed out (ie muting a module to see if it improves anything then investigate if it does) but for the most part they are too heavy-handed and not sympathetic to the music. Presumably it must accidentally get some things right occasionally?

I bailed out of the Ozone upgrade train at V9 because some of this automatic stuff is getting too intrusive – I actually prefer some of the V8 modules. As more (so-called) AI has been poured into Ozone it has got worse and the ability to use third party plugins in the chain was removed at V10, which I do all the time. IMHO (and it’s only my opinion) there are no shortcuts in this game – doing stuff and listening critically is the way you learn – more than watching a million videos or prodding AI assistance buttons which suddenly chuck 5, 8, 10 processing modules (MudYouAlls?) into your chain to confuse the situation! As always YMMV.

I do know someone that makes heavy use of all this stuff, he’s not particularly experienced and invariably he’s trying to fix something he should have addressed in the mix – and that’s the downside with this stuff, less experienced people think mastering is about turd polishing – the turd should already be shiny before you attempt to polish it further. I have taken him back to his mix and worked through his (perceived) master issues several times – I hope he’ll be weaned off the magic button within a month or two! (With my luck just in time for V12 which changes the mastering world as we know it!)

I'll basically agree with that. There are some processors in each that would have been unimaginable 10 years ago.

I also really like a couple of specific settings for the Ozone Maximizer; you can set it to get some really nice, subtle distortion. Or, you can set it to be a lot more "transparent". Quote:
Originally Posted by Coconut_Head ➡️
For seeing what steps the AI assistant does (as others here have said they do), are there any better alternatives?

Also is the AI assistant the same in the Standard version as it is in Advanced? Or can I not see what it's doing to the individual modules in Standard? I do understand that I can't change them in Standard.
I don't know, I've only ever used Advanced because using a few individual modules as their own plugins was the draw for me.

But, asking for better alternatives on a mastering forum sounds a lot like you're asking session guitarists what plugin will replace them on a guitar forum. I don't have any suggestions because I honestly don't think an AI will ever do that as well as a half-decent human mastering engineer.

All the "assistants" are ever going to do is measure your track in a handful of ways and figure out processors to make it closer to an average of a handful (or a lot) of other tracks, just like how all LLMs or Generators are going to do is figure out the output that would likely answer or be described by the prompt based on the stuff it has available to train on.

In a very fundamental way, I believe that AI is never going to be better at creating art than halfway talented humans.

If you're not going to send it to someone to master, I honestly think the best thing to do is to focus 100% of your attention on getting the mix to sound as good as you possibly can on several different sound systems and learn one good limiter (with a level-matched preview) inside and out to get it up to the loudness you want.

Pro-L2 is probably the easiest to learn IMHO, and it's 1:1 mode is very good. The Ozone Maximizer might be easier to learn for some people (and it's gain match is almost as good)...it depends on how you think about things. The big difference, other than that they sound different and create different patterns of harmonics, is that they name things differently either based on what they're doing at a technical level or what they're supposed to accomplish...and some of the names don't make any sense (e.g., attack vs transient emphasis, neither of which is the same as attack on a compressor...release vs character...aggressive/pumping/safe/etc vs crisp/clipping/modern/etc).

Originally Posted by Ghost ➡️
This reminds me, for some reason I keep hearing Ozone's upward compression removing sub energy. Ive confirmed this on many genres and im convinced it's a bug or something unwanted that snuck by them. I want to use it because otherwise I think it works well. Can you or anyone else hear this happening?
It definitely does. Its an automatic setting for their EDM AI too, which is wild. Seems to really deflate things sometimes.

Another thought about the assistant: A lot of the settings the assistant gives are automatic to the genre and aren't relative to the track.

Since I use EDM the most, I'll go with that. These are automatic settings every time you run the assistant set to EDM:

- Clarity is always set to 25 no tilt
- Stabilizer is always set to 25 with low/mid/high at 100
- Upward compression is always set to +2.7
- Soft clipping is never enabled
- Transient emphasis is never enabled
- Maximizer always set to IRC 4 Modern with character set to 2.0.

What's actually relative to the track:

- Equalizer, which it can get pretty drastic with, and can change drastically if you start the assistant sampling just a few seconds earlier or later in the track. Usually overcooks, though in the right direction, so you can turn down what it does and its usually fairly on point
- Imager, seems fine to me, but I usually turn this off
- Impact, seems fine to me but I usually turn this off
- Dynamic EQ, which is risky because it seems to just notch whatever the loudest three frequencies are in that section of the track, which might actually be important and on top for a reason, and the notches then carry over to the rest of the track. I usually turn this off.

In the end, I end up using the Equalizer, Clarity under 10, Stabilizer under 10, and Maximizer with upward compression off, and soft clip under 10 (usually around 5 or 6.)

All in all, as I'm mostly concerned with mastering my own demos, I mostly use the assistant for mixdown feedback. I'll run it to gain some "ears" on the track, look over what it suggests (from the things that aren't automatic) and if I agree with its assessment, will address it in the mix.
