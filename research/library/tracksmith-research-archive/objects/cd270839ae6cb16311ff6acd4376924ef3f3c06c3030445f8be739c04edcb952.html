Mastering The Mix Announces Faster Master
by The Press Desk   27th August 2025

Introducing Faster Master, a straightforward mastering plugin co-created with David Guetta and Nicky Romero.


What Is FASTER MASTER?

FASTER MASTER analyzes your song and delivers 10 custom-tailored presets that give you a professional starting point. Pick the vibe that fits best, then fine-tune the details to make it your own.

Every adjustment is automatically level-matched, so you hear the real impact of your changes without being fooled by volume differences, leading to more confident, pro-level decisions.
  • Get professional, release-ready masters fast.
  • Every move is level-matched, so your decisions are always honest.
  • Import reference tracks and instantly match their sonic profile.
  • Smart presets adapt to your song for tailored results every time.

I've been testing FM and the new Ozone 12 Mastering Assistant for several hours now. Here's my first impressions, and I'd like to ask you guys if you share my feelings. A HUGE caveat: I've been testing ONLY on acoustic, folk and country music, for now. I am pretty sure that I would have somewhat different impressions with different genres. Not sure. You tell me.
1. FM has a huge advantage in price, obviously.
2. Ozone MA has a significant advantage in tweakability and control, obviously. And that is reflected in the price. Ozone 12 and FM are not really comparable, and that's not my point. What I am comparing is Ozone 12 MA (only the MA feature) and FM.
So these are obvious differences. What I am mostly interested, though, is the quality that one can achieve with a QUICK result, automatic or minimal tweaking, that both tools aim at (again, I am specifically referring to Ozone MA, not the whole Ozone package). And here's my sensations
3. FM provides a range of "starting points" (smart presets) that is much, much wider. For example, I normally get both really, really, super-bright smart presets and, at the same time, much darker ones. Completely, radically different. Some very good, some completely useless. Then it's up to me to choose. Now, I don't think this is a good thing, at all. It's not real guidance, it's disorientation. If the goal is to help the user to get to a very quick, automatic or semi-automatic good or very good mastering chain, then providing a huge range of possible outcomes defies the purpose, even if one sees them as just starting points, as one should. Especially because that huge variety, from useless to very good, generate a feel of randomness about the whole process, which is the opposite of "smartness". Not what I want from a "smart" tool.
Ozone MA, on the contrary, only provides ONE chain (within the same genre), and that's NOT ideal either. Ideally, I would like the software to provide just very few potential chains (2 or 3 or 4 at the most) that are NOT too different so that the user is "guided" towards a good solution, but he/she can also choose depending on taste without spending hours in tweaking.
4. On balance, though, I do prefer the stronger "orientation" provided by Ozone MA. Mostly, because it seems to always provide a very, very, very good starting point, on par or better than even the best smart presets produced by FM. I found myself tweaking very little and very quickly with Ozone MA. Quickly being the keyword here. Of course I can improve everything if I want to spend hours and hours on a mastering project. But this is not the goal I'm talking about. This is not the goal of neither FM or Ozone MA. When I want / can spend hours in mastering, I don't need these tools.
Second, I tend to prefer Ozone MA because the tweaking capability is much more powerful and flexibile than FM. Ideally, I don't need much tweaking, as I said, but it's undeniable that it's good to be able to do it. The tweaking that FM provides is very limited. Almost everything is "under the hood", outside of the user's reach. This is not to say that what happens under the hood in FM is not complex or sophisticated, I'm sure it is. I like MTM a lot, I own and use most of their plugins. But FM just doesn't provide the control that Ozone MA does, simple as that. Again, ideally with these tools the necessary tweaking should be minimal. Paradoxically, the control given to the user is higher in Ozone MA even though there is LESS NEED for tweaking because of the consistency and quality of its results.
5. Obviously, the huge price difference is a big factor in favor of FM, depending on your budget obviously. But I feel that the price difference is somewhat justified. I am not saying that FM is a bad product, at all. It's excellent considering the price, and considering its limitations, that are - at the moment - significant compared to Ozone MA.
What will I buy? I am not sure. I need to try both with other genres first.

For example, I normally get both really, really, super-bright smart presets and, at the same time, much darker ones. Completely, radically different. Some very good, some completely useless. Then it's up to me to choose. Now, I don't think this is a good thing, at all. It's not real guidance, it's disorientation. If the goal is to help the user to get to a very quick, automatic or semi-automatic good or very good mastering chain, then providing a huge range of possible outcomes defies the purpose, even if one sees them as just starting points, as one should. Especially because that huge variety, from useless to very good, generate a feel of randomness about the whole process, which is the opposite of "smartness". Not what I want from a "smart" tool.
Ozone MA, on the contrary, only provides ONE chain (within the same genre), and that's NOT ideal either. Ideally, I would like the software to provide just very few potential chains (2 or 3 or 4 at the most) that are NOT too different so that the user is "guided" towards a good solution, but he/she can also choose depending on taste without spending hours in tweaking.
4. On balance, though, I do prefer the stronger "orientation" provided by Ozone MA. Mostly, because it seems to always provide a very, very, very good starting point, on par or better than even the best smart presets produced by FM. I found myself tweaking very little and very quickly with Ozone MA. Quickly being the keyword here. Of course I can improve everything if I want to spend hours and hours on a mastering project. But this is not the goal I'm talking about. This is not the goal of neither FM or Ozone MA. When I want / can spend hours in mastering, I don't need these tools.
Second, I tend to prefer Ozone MA because the tweaking capability is much more powerful and flexibile than FM. Ideally, I don't need much tweaking, as I said, but it's undeniable that it's good to be able to do it. The tweaking that FM provides is very limited. Almost everything is "under the hood", outside of the user's reach. This is not to say that what happens under the hood in FM is not complex or sophisticated, I'm sure it is. I like MTM a lot, I own and use most of their plugins. But FM just doesn't provide the control that Ozone MA does, simple as that. Again, ideally with these tools the necessary tweaking should be minimal. Paradoxically, the control given to the user is higher in Ozone MA even though there is LESS NEED for tweaking because of the consistency and quality of its results.
5. Obviously, the huge price difference is a big factor in favor of FM, depending on your budget obviously. But I feel that the price difference is somewhat justified. I am not saying that FM is a bad product, at all. It's excellent considering the price, and considering its limitations, that are - at the moment - significant compared to Ozone MA.
What will I buy? I am not sure. I need to try both with other genres first.
Comparing FM with O12 highlights decision paralysis. The concept behind FM is selective options with limited processing range to get you to a mastered version fast.

You can now do the same in O12 by limiting the options before analysis, but the cost difference is vast between the 2 plugins, while the results are comparable.

I'd compared FM to IK's Master Match. Similar in price and functionality.
