# TrackSmith Logic core-effect mastery priority

Version: 2026-07-18.2  
Machine-readable record: `research/knowledge/logic-pro-12.3-core-effect-priority.json`

## Priority correction

Pedalboard and Bitcrusher were examples, not the center of ordinary production.
TrackSmith retains their complete documentary catalog and the first bounded
Bitcrusher host record, but further deep work now follows broad production role:
gain/balance, equalization, dynamics, cleanup, ambience, delay, harmonic color,
limiting/metering, stereo/phase, and source-appropriate pitch work come first.

This is a TrackSmith product/research priority, not a claim that Apple publishes
global usage telemetry or that a creative processor is artistically less valuable.
A ring modulator may be exactly right for one song; it simply covers fewer routine
requests than Channel EQ or Compressor.

The same generated queue now supplies the companion's typed context rank. It is
used only as a semantic-match tie-breaker; explicit effect names and aliases still
dominate. Every supplied core rank carries the machine-readable boundary
`CURATED_58_CASE_ROLE_RECURRENCE_NOT_GLOBAL_USAGE_TELEMETRY_OR_FIXED_PROCESSING_ORDER`.
Generic source words and ordinary stopwords are excluded from single-token effect
identity scoring, so “this guitar” cannot silently become an Amp/Pedalboard request
and “the” cannot retrieve a pedal named *The Vibe*. Source-specific repair and
delivery tools also require their relevant concept unless explicitly named:
*airy*, *controlled*, or *polished* alone cannot imply de-essing, a noise gate,
pitch correction, limiting, or a loudness meter.

The 400-plus semantic/adversarial cases add coverage pressure rather than a
frequency estimate: terms such as clear, muddy, punchy, controlled, intimate,
distant, wide, sibilant, warm, and aggressive require these core roles to be
considered without reducing any term to a fixed effect.

## First mastery lanes

1. **Gain, balance, routing, and meters.** Logic Gain, Level Meter, Loudness
   Meter, MultiMeter, Correlation Meter, and Direction Mixer. Every A/B and every
   downstream effect claim depends on a correct level/channel baseline.
2. **Equalization.** Channel EQ first, then Linear Phase and Single Band as
   alternatives. TrackSmith must distinguish broad tone, masking, resonance,
   filtering, headroom, and phase/transient consequences.
3. **Compression and source dynamics.** Compressor first, then DeEsser 2, Noise
   Gate, Enveloper, Expander, and Multipressor. Static curves, detectors,
   ballistics, makeup gain, event specificity, and preservation are separate.
4. **Reverb and depth.** ChromaVerb and Space Designer first. Predelay, early/
   late balance, decay spectrum, direct/effect ratio, modulation, distance, stereo,
   and source masking must stay distinct.
5. **Delay.** Stereo Delay and Tape Delay first, followed by Delay Designer,
   Echo, and Sample Delay. Timing, sync, feedback, filtering, saturation, tails,
   width, and rhythmic placement are different dimensions.
6. **Saturation and harmonic color.** ChromaGlow and Overdrive first, then Clip
   Distortion and the other distortion models. Level-matched harmonic, IMD,
   transient, alias, noise, and low-end consequences must be explicit.
7. **Limiting, loudness, and delivery.** Limiter and Adaptive Limiter alongside
   untouched ITU/EBU standards and TrackSmith's own meters. Apple mastering
   suggestions do not become universal targets.
8. **Stereo, phase, and mono translation.** Direction Mixer, Correlation Meter,
   Stereo Spread, and Sample Delay, with channel-matrix and frequency-dependent
   mono checks.
9. **Pitch correction and transformation.** Pitch Correction first, conditioned
   on source, scale/key, confidence, polyphony, formants, performance intent, and
   audible artifacts.
10. **Creative/specialized color.** Bitcrusher, Pedalboard, Step FX, Phat FX,
    Remix FX, ring modulation, and similar tools remain fully cataloged but follow
    the core lanes.

## Definition of “mastered” for a core Logic effect

An effect is not mastered because its manual page was read or because one preset
rendered. For each high-priority identity TrackSmith needs:

- exact Logic/build, manual payload, page, controls, modes, routing, latency/tail,
  automation/state, and documentation contradictions;
- direct host default/bypass and a processor-specific bounded parameter campaign;
- input-level, sample-rate, mono/stereo, repeatability, save/reload, and source-
  preservation evidence where applicable;
- measured transfer, spectrum, envelope, timing, stereo, peak, loudness, and
  nonfinite results only for dimensions the method supports;
- level-matched audiovisual review on provenance-recorded vocal, drum, bass,
  guitar, synth/keys, and full-mix material where applicable;
- source-specific production hypotheses, alternatives, preservation risks,
  stopping conditions, and cases where no processing is the better choice;
- ordinary musician language, ambiguous language, contraindications, and
  multi-turn revisions in the evaluation corpus;
- a strict boundary between Apple-documented behavior, TrackSmith measurement,
  professional-practice heuristic, and subjective preference.

The next direct-host processing queue is therefore the remaining Channel EQ grid,
then Compressor,
DeEsser 2, Noise Gate, ChromaVerb, Space Designer, Stereo Delay, Tape Delay,
ChromaGlow, Limiter, Direction Mixer, and Pitch Correction—not more Pedalboard
work. The first partial Channel EQ default/bypass/1 kHz-bell run does not close the
processor. Gain/metering validation remains a dependency throughout.

## Implemented campaign consequence

The empirical campaign no longer assigns those identities only a generic family
sweep. It now fails closed on processor-specific dimensions. Examples:

- Channel EQ requires every band/filter type, slope, frequency/gain/Q states,
  magnitude/phase/group delay, coupled bands, headroom, L/R/M/S behavior,
  oversampling, analyzer audio-null, and save/reload evidence.
- Compressor requires static curve, makeup/Auto Gain, attack/release/recovery,
  Peak/RMS and side-chain behavior, all seven models, coloration/limiter/mix,
  stereo linking, and source-preservation listening.
- DeEsser 2 distinguishes short consonant-like events from static brightness and
  requires Relative/Absolute, Split/Wide, frequency/range, maximum reduction,
  stereo-event, breath, air, intelligibility, and lisp-risk checks.
- Reverbs and delays require impulse/tail, early/late or repeat timing, spectral
  decay, feedback, modulation, stereo/mono, transition, real-time/offline, and
  source-articulation evidence as applicable.
- Saturation, drive, and limiting require level-matched transfer, harmonic/IMD/
  alias/noise, transient/dynamic, peak, true-peak, sample-rate, and artifact
  checks rather than adjective-based judgments.
- Stereo and pitch tools require explicit channel matrices or monophonic tracking
  boundaries, known failure material, and listening-based identity preservation.

These dimensions are machine-validated in
`logic-pro-12.3-empirical-campaign.json`. A partial run may close only a declared
subset; a `complete` run must close every declared generic and core-specific
dimension. That prevents a single preset render from becoming “effect mastered.”
